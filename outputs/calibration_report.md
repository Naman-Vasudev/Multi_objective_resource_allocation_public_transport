**Explanations:**
- **Route 37.** Night service (21:10-03:50), 32 km, 50 min headway, 2 buses. Two buses can run a 50 min headway on a 64 km round trip only at about 38 km/h (no layover). That speed is possible on empty night roads, but it is far above 22 km/h. The sheet also lists only one direction. Either night speed is much higher, or 32 km is already the round trip. Needs CTU confirmation.
- **Route 40.** ISBT-17 to Khizrabad, 30.8 km, 60 min headway, 3 buses. Short only once 10% layover is added (needs 22.6 km/h vs 22). A marginal case: a slightly faster suburban speed or a shorter layover explains it.
- **Route 76.** ISBT-17 to Kurali, 31 km, 30-40 min headway, 4 buses, only 2 via-stops listed: a suburban or highway run with few stops. It needs about 23 km/h (no layover) or 26 km/h (10% layover), which is believable for a route with so few stops. Explained by speed, not an error.

## 4. Integer check: x_base vs ceil(f·τ)

`x_base < ceil(f_min·τ)` means the route cannot run even its slowest published frequency with its own buses at the
assumed speed. `x_base > ceil(f_max·τ)` means it has more buses than its fastest published frequency needs.
The counts for every combination are in the table above. Read across scenarios:

- **S1 (d_r = full cycle) is implausible.** At 22 km/h, 57 of 68 routes would hold more buses than needed
  even at their highest frequency, and only 44% of the fleet would be in use. A transit
  operator does not leave about half its fleet idle. This is strong evidence that d_r is a one-way length.
- **S2 at 18 km/h** fills the fleet (with no layover) but leaves 23 routes unable to run their published
  minimum frequency, which contradicts the timetable CTU actually operates. 18 km/h is too slow as a network-wide speed.
- **S2 at 22 km/h with 10% layover** (recommended): 3 route(s) short (37, 40, 76) and 13 route(s) with
  surplus buses (10, 17, 22, 23A, 35, 35B, 71, 80, 85, 123A, 551, 240, 38AB). Surplus buses are expected. They usually mean the route is slower than average (city
  core, ISBT and PGI approaches), has longer terminal layovers, runs extra short trips not in the headway
  (the sheet notes these for routes 20 and 242), or carries the depot's peak and spare buses. A route-level speed
  would remove most of these; that needs CTU data we do not have.

**Short-span and night routes.** 4 routes are not active in the AM peak (11A, 24A, 24C, 37;
5 buses). If their buses were shared with day routes, the simultaneous requirement under S2 at 22 km/h with 10% layover would
be 334.9 instead of 340.9. The difference is small, so the model treats route allocations as separate.

## 6. Recommendation (PENDING STUDENT CONFIRMATION)

Use **S2 distance reading + v = 22 km/h + 10% layover + 10% spares** (S3 at 22 km/h, 10%):
N_total = 375.0 buses = 96.4% of B. Evidence:
1. It is in the 85-100% band, as an operator's baseline should be.
2. Among the in-band options it has the fewest per-route contradictions (3 routes), except S3 with
   no layover, which is physically unrealistic because drivers need terminal time.
3. It keeps the workbook's own 22 km/h assumption, so it is consistent with the existing sheet.
4. S1 leaves about half the fleet idle and over-allocates most routes, so it is rejected.

In the optimisation model this means τ_r = (2·d_r if non-loop else d_r) / 22 × 1.10 [h], with fleet constraint
Σ_r f_r · τ_r ≤ B / (1 + s) = 353.6 buses in service. See `docs/DECISIONS.md` D2-D6.
